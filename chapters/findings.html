Chapter · Findings

What held up across the cases.

The demos show useful workflows for documents, answers and decisions. A real deployment tests them with a customer’s own documents, people, connections and operating controls.

For: partners and customers deciding what evidence they still need.

The findings below show what held up across the cases and what to settle before using real data.

The pattern that held up

Keep language, rules and decisions separate.

Ingram Micro and its partners want AI that does real work in five areas: knowledge, customer engagement, analytics, document processing and content. iMBrace's pitch is one platform where messages, documents, data, decisions and actions meet. We found that each link in that chain works on its own (a web chat reaches an agent, a document becomes a record, a record feeds an answer, a flow waits for a decision), while the operating view is spread across several screens. Agree how a team will join those records when it investigates a task.

Most of the finished cases share one shape:

  • The AI handles the language. It finds the clause, drafts the reply, writes the copy, words the alert.
  • Fixed steps do the sums and enforce the rules. FlowOps code steps add up the totals, check every figure the AI writes, match an order number with its email, and compare the output with rules kept as records on a board. These are labelled "Built on iMBrace with FlowOps".
  • The workflow holds for a decision. An approval step was exercised by the automated demo operator. The custom workflow records the exact version approved; a real deployment must add and test distinct approver identities.
  • The team can inspect the work in FlowOps Runs, DataIQ records boards and DocIQ document logs (See everything in one place).

What that looked like

These examples use fictional data and labelled demo finance or ERP boards. The automated demo operator plays customer and approver roles.

  • PA-1 Invoice and claims captureFour invoices and a claim form go in. The clean invoice and the clean claim reach the demo finance board automatically; three invoices wait for a person, each with its reason in plain words; the same invoice sent twice is stopped.
  • KM-1 HR policy Q&AAsked whether 10 days of leave can still be carried over until June, the assistant gives the rule in force (5 days, used by 31 March) and cites the document, version and section.
  • CA-3 Sales and margin Q&AEast's Q3 gross margin is $131,600, 32.7% of revenue, with its working. When a cell on the board is mistyped, the next answer names the bad record instead of adding it in.
  • CG-1 Marketing contentA sales manager's off-brand draft is checked against brand rules marketing keeps on a board; every problem is named with its rule, the copy is rewritten on brand, and the workflow holds it for a marketing approval played by the automated demo operator.
  • CE-5 One agent, every front doorThe same cited answer comes back through iMBrace's console, its hosted web chat and three doors on a demo partner portal.

Where to start

Choose a workflow with a clear source and a clear decision.

Useful starting points

  • Internal assistants that answer from documents and boards, cite their source and say when they don't know (KM-1, KM-3, CA-3).
  • Document intake where clean documents go straight through and people handle the exceptions (PA-1).
  • Workflows that wait for an approver or a customer's reply and pick up where they stopped (CG-1, CG-2 and PA-3).
  • One assistant offered to staff, customers and partners through different front doors (CE-5).

Each was shown on synthetic data. First agree access and data controls, then test representative customer documents: KM-3's runbooks, for example, had to be re-laid out one step per line before iMBrace's ingestion read them reliably.

What to settle before using real data

  • Native safeguards. No guardrail provider was provisioned, including after the account became Admin. Custom FlowOps phrase and version checks are separate from native guardrails. Ask iMBrace which supported components a deployment will use and test the actual policy behaviour; the guardrail case remains dropped.
  • Separate roles. One login played every role, and the account belonged to no team. Use distinct accounts to test correction, approval, team inbox access and answers by audience.
  • Data location. The demonstrated model route used AWS US West (Oregon). The platform and log locations were not verified. Obtain a data-flow and hosting map for the customer's data-protection review before using confidential data.
  • Cost and tracing. Token Usage omitted most case assistants and DocIQ reads; partial AI traces do not follow every step. Zero usage is not zero cost. Agree metering, billing reconciliation and a way to correlate records across screens.
  • Real connections. ERP and CRM write-back were not tested. A later demo mailbox connection was read-only, and an approved reply path was not demonstrated. The observed FlowOps library included NetSuite, Salesforce, ServiceNow and Dynamics pieces, but none for SAP. Verify the customer's actual connector and sandbox delivery path; a catalogue entry alone is not an integration.
  • Representative documents and volume. Most cases used small scripted cohorts; PA-1 also retained a 200-invoice run. Answers took about 5 seconds to just over a minute. These observations do not establish production accuracy, concurrency or scale. Agree a document set, baseline, accuracy target and load test.
  • Security. Agree a security review with iMBrace before real customer or employee data is used.

Benefits we couldn't test or show

Unavailable in the tested configuration (10)

Guardrails, choice of safety model, prompt-injection defence and PII masking all need a provider that was not provisioned on this tenant; identity-provider group mapping; live human takeover and one inbox across channels (team-scoped, and the account is in no team); classifying and routing conversations; CRM or ERP write-back; and templates.

Present, untested (7)

Comment moderation; team-scoped knowledge; role-based permissions; field-level access; credentials kept out of workflows; offline email fallback; SLA chips. Most need a second user or a team; offline fallback remains unproven despite the later read-only demo Gmail connection.

Not in our UI (7)

An immutable ledger; a business record of events; overrides recorded with a reason; a lifecycle workspace; case SLA management; infrastructure health (on-premises only, per the vendor); SOP-drift optimisation.

Not demonstrable (7)

Every regulated message policy-checked; security-trimmed retrieval; encryption and tenant isolation; growth analytics; no training on customer data (and the vendor's own wording conflicts); security attestations; regulatory alignment. These are claims or legal statements with nothing to show on screen.

How sure we are

What this evidence covers

  • One demo account, initially Builder and later changed to Admin by another account outside the run. It remained in no team, so role separation and team behaviour are not demonstrated.
  • Synthetic data and scripted scenarios: most cases run small cohorts; PA-1 adds a retained 200-invoice depth run. That shows designed behaviour, not an accuracy or production-scale rate.
  • One model, DeepSeek V3.2 on iMBrace's built-in provider. Claude and OpenAI models refused calls from this account's region.
  • A point in time: iMBrace version 2026.10.30, observed from 4 to 6 November 2026.
  • One login played every role. The demos label automated customer and approver actions; they do not prove separation of duties.
  • Buying demand is a separate question. The 6 November demand record supports categories and ranks customer care, invoice capture and sales analytics highly. Exact buying demand for the order desk, competitive response, five-front-door and multi-agent demonstrations is unverified. Ask the buyer about its current volume, cost and decision criteria; vendor outcome percentages do not forecast an iMBrace result.
